# Credits

Portier was made with love ❤️, stubborn engineering taste, and a frankly unreasonable amount of AI assistance.

This project is **AI-assisted**, not AI-abandoned.

The operating model was human-in-the-loop from the beginning: a human producer/director set the product direction, made the architectural calls, rejected bad ideas, approved scope, pushed for tests, demanded audits, and generally kept the machines from sprinting confidently into a wall.

## End Credits

**Director / Producer / Human In The Loop**  
The maintainer — steering the product, deciding what matters, and asking the annoying-but-necessary questions.

**Lead Development Assistant**  
Claude — implementation accomplice, refactoring goblin, test-writing machine, and occasional overenthusiastic architect.

**Prompt Department / Second Unit / Structured Chaos Support**  
Codex — prompt drafting, task slicing, review support, and making sure the next instruction had enough rails that the robots did not invent a monorail.

**Quality Department**  
Many, many validation runs, audits, contract checks, coverage gates, and the ancient engineering ritual of asking: “but what breaks when this fails?”

## How To Read This

The AI helped with speed, breadth, iteration, code, tests, docs, and structured review.

The human drove the intent.

That distinction matters.

Portier is a small example of what modern software work can look like when AI is treated as a powerful collaborator rather than an autopilot: fast, useful, occasionally weird, and still accountable to a person with taste, context, and the final say.

No sentient machines were harmed during development.  
Several were asked to rerun the tests.
